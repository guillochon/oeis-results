## What is counted
a(n) is the number of n × n symmetric positive definite matrices M with every diagonal entry 2 and every
off-diagonal entry in {−1, 0, 1}. The **graph** of M has vertices 1..n and an edge ij when M(i,j) ≠ 0; M is
**connected** when its graph is. c(k) denotes the number of connected such matrices of size k
(c(1), c(2), … = 1, 2, 16, 304, 11008, …; this sequence is not in the OEIS).

## The formula
**Theorem.** With a(0) = 1,

    Sum_{n>=0} a(n) x^n/n! = exp(C(x)),   C(x) = Sum_{k>=1} c(k) x^k/k!,   c(k) = c_A(k) + c_D(k) + c_E(k),

where
- c_A(1) = 1, and c_A(k) = 2^(k−1)·(k+1)^(k−2) for k ≥ 2;
- c_D(k) = 0 for k ≤ 3, c_D(4) = 104, and for k ≥ 5
  c_D(k) = 2^(k−1)·U(k) + (k−1)·k^(k−2)·2^(k−2) = A320064(k) = 2^(k−2)·A000435(k),
  where U(k) = A057500(k) is the number of labelled connected unicyclic graphs on k vertices;
- c_E(6) = 355104, c_E(7) = 43733504, c_E(8) = 4053973888, and c_E(k) = 0 otherwise.

Equivalently, a(n) = Sum_{k=1..n} binomial(n−1, k−1)·c(k)·a(n−k), with a(0) = 1.

**Closed form.** Let T(z) = Sum_{m≥1} m^(m−1) z^m/m! be the tree function (e.g.f. of A000169; T = z·e^T), and
t = T(2x). Then

    C(x) = (t − t²/2)/(4x) − t/4 − log(1−t)/4 − 1/2 + x/2 − x²/2 − 8x³/3 − 26x⁴/3
           + 355104 x⁶/6! + 43733504 x⁷/7! + 4053973888 x⁸/8!.

The A part is (t − t²/2 − 2x)/(4x) + x/2 (spanning trees; the x/2 fixes k = 1). The D part is
½U(2x) + t²/8 − x²/2 − 16x³/3! − 208x⁴/4! = −log(1−t)/4 − t/4 − x²/2 − 8x³/3 − 26x⁴/3. Here
U(z) = −½log(1−T) − T/2 − T²/4 is the e.g.f. of A057500 (cycle of length ≥ 3), t²/8 counts the double-edge case
(trees with a marked edge have e.g.f. T²/2), and the polynomial removes k = 2 and k = 3 (D_2 = A_1 + A_1,
D_3 = A_3) and applies the D_4 triality factor ([`proofs.md`](proofs.md) §5).

**Asymptotics.** a(n) ~ K · n! · (2e)^n · n^(−7/8), where

    K = exp(e/4 − 3/4 + 1/(4e) − 1/(8e²) − 1/(3e³) − 13/(24e⁴)
            + 355104/(6!(2e)⁶) + 43733504/(7!(2e)⁷) + 4053973888/(8!(2e)⁸)) / (2^(1/8)·Γ(1/8))
      = 0.14730039…

This comes from the square-root singularity of T at z = 1/e. With x₀ = 1/(2e), the only divergent part of C is
−log(1−t)/4, and exp of it is (1−t)^(−1/4) ~ 2^(−1/8)(1 − x/x₀)^(−1/8). The rest of C, evaluated at x₀ (t = 1), gives
the exponent in K, and singularity transfer with exponent 1/8 gives n^(−7/8). The relative correction is O(n^(−1/2)),
so a(n)/(leading term) approaches 1 slowly (see Checks).

## Why it is true (proof sketch; full proof in [`proofs.md`](proofs.md))
1. **Matrices are root bases.** M is such a matrix iff it is the Gram matrix of a basis b_1..b_n of a lattice in
   which every b_i is a root (a vector of norm 2). That lattice is even and generated by roots, so by **Witt's
   theorem** it is an orthogonal sum of root lattices A_k (k ≥ 1), D_k (k ≥ 4), E_6, E_7, E_8.
2. **Components.** The connected components of the graph of M correspond to the irreducible summands. So M is block
   diagonal with connected blocks, which gives the exponential formula a = exp(C) above.
3. **Counting by lattice.** For an irreducible root lattice L of rank k, the number of k × k matrices whose
   lattice is isometric to L is (number of ordered bases of L made of roots) / |Aut(L)|: Aut(L) acts freely on
   bases, and two bases have the same Gram matrix iff an automorphism maps one to the other.
   |Aut(A_k)| = 2(k+1)! (k ≥ 2), |Aut(D_k)| = 2^k·k! (k ≥ 5; 3 times that for D_4), |Aut(E_6)| = 103680,
   |Aut(E_7)| = 2903040, |Aut(E_8)| = 696729600.
4. **A_k** (roots ±(e_i − e_j) in Z^(k+1)). k roots form a basis iff their edges form a spanning tree of K_(k+1),
   each edge in either orientation. Cayley's (k+1)^(k−1) trees (A000272) give c_A(k).
5. **D_k** (roots ±e_i ± e_j in Z^k). k roots form a basis iff the signed multigraph they define on the k
   coordinates is connected with k edges and its unique cycle has negative sign product (an "unbalanced" cycle,
   possibly a double edge). These are the reflectable bases counted by A320064, which gives c_D(k) for k ≥ 5.
6. **E_6, E_7, E_8.** Each contributes one constant, a finite computation (next section). There are no E-type
   components beyond size 8, so every term follows from c_A, c_D and the three constants.

The A_k and D_k basis characterizations (steps 4 and 5) are also formalized in Lean 4 / Mathlib
(`lean/OeisLean/A114601.lean`: `span_eq_A_iff`, `span_eq_D_iff`; standard axioms only). Witt's theorem, the
automorphism group orders, the tree and unicyclic counts and the E constants are not formalized.

## How the E constants were computed (two independent routes, which agree)
- **Route 1, all matrices** (`rust/`, `a114601 gram k`): enumerate every positive definite matrix of size k by
  depth-first search over rows, with exact integer determinants, and keep the connected ones of determinant
  det(E_k) = 3, 2, 1. For k = 8 (68 s) the connected matrices split into det 1 (E_8) 4053973888, det 4 (D_8)
  301321216 and det 9 (A_8) 68024448; the last two equal c_D(8) and c_A(8).
- **Route 2, actual roots** (`a114601 roots E6|E7|E8`): take the roots of E_8 in standard coordinates (E_7 and E_6
  as the roots orthogonal to one root and to an A_2), count the k-subsets of positive roots whose Gram determinant
  is det(E_k), i.e. the bases, and divide the ordered count by |Aut(E_k)|. The whole enumeration is checked by
  Cauchy–Binet: the sum of det over all k-subsets must equal h^k, with Coxeter number h = 12, 18, 30.
  For E_8 only subsets containing a fixed root are enumerated (the Weyl group is transitive on roots) and the
  count is scaled back; this shortcut was validated on E_7.

  these bumps die out; without the E terms the ratio is smooth.

## A short program
PARI/GP, straight from the closed form. a(8)..a(18) take milliseconds and a(200) about 3 s:

```
a(n)=my(x='x+O('x^(n+2)),t=subst(serreverse(x/exp(x)),'x,2*x));n!*polcoef(exp((t-t^2/2)/(4*x)-t/4-log(1-t)/4-1/2+x/2-x^2/2-8*x^3/3-26*x^4/3+355104*x^6/6!+43733504*x^7/7!+4053973888*x^8/8!),n)
```

## The idea: count root bases lattice by lattice
Let M be such a matrix. Then M is the Gram matrix of n linearly independent vectors of norm 2 with pairwise inner products in {−1, 0, 1}. These vectors generate an integral lattice L of rank n spanned by norm-2 vectors. So:

- **L is a root lattice**, a direct sum of copies of A_k, D_k, E_6, E_7 and E_8.
- The n vectors are a **basis of L consisting of roots**.
- Conversely, any basis of an ADE lattice made of roots works. Distinct roots that are not opposite have inner product in {−1, 0, 1}, and the Gram matrix of a basis is positive definite.
- Two ordered bases give the same M iff an isometry of L maps one to the other. Aut(L) acts freely on bases, so
  **#(matrices with lattice L) = #(ordered root bases of L) / |Aut(L)|.**
- M is block diagonal along the connected components of its graph of nonzero entries, and a connected M has an irreducible L. So with c(k) = the number of *connected* matrices of size k:

  **a(n) = n! [xⁿ] exp( Σ_k c(k) xᵏ / k! ),   c(k) = c_A(k) + c_D(k) + c_E(k).**

### The three families
- **A_k** (roots e_i − e_j in Z^(k+1)). A set of k roots is a basis iff the corresponding edges form a spanning tree of K_(k+1), in either orientation. That gives 2ᵏ(k+1)ᵏ⁻¹ sets. With |Aut(A_k)| = 2(k+1)! for k ≥ 2:
  **c_A(k) = 2ᵏ⁻¹(k+1)ᵏ⁻²** (k ≥ 2), and c_A(1) = 1.
- **D_k** (roots ±e_i ± e_j in Zᵏ, k ≥ 4). A set of k roots is a basis iff the corresponding signed multigraph on the k coordinates is connected and unicyclic, and its cycle is *unbalanced* (the roots are independent). The claim is that it then generates all of D_k, not a sublattice of index > 1.
  - Cycles of length ≥ 3: U(k)·4ᵏ/2 sets, where U(k) = A057500(k) counts labelled connected unicyclic graphs.
  - A double edge (both e_i + e_j and e_i − e_j): (k−1)k^(k−2)·4^(k−1) sets.
  - With |Aut(D_k)| = 2ᵏk! for k ≥ 5, and 3 times that for k = 4 (triality):
  **c_D(k) = 2ᵏ⁻¹U(k) + (k−1)kᵏ⁻²2ᵏ⁻²** for k ≥ 5, and c_D(4) = 104. D_3 = A_3 is not counted twice.
- **E_6, E_7, E_8**: one constant each, in ranks 6, 7 and 8 only. **For n ≥ 9 no new E terms appear.** Every term then follows from the formula and the three constants.
