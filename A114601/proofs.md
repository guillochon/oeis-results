# A114601: proof of the formula

**Theorem.** Let a(n) be the number of n × n symmetric positive definite matrices with every diagonal entry 2 and every off-diagonal entry in {−1, 0, 1}, with a(0) = 1. Then

  **a(n) = n! [xⁿ] exp C(x),   C(x) = Σ_{k≥1} c(k) xᵏ/k!,   c(k) = c_A(k) + c_D(k) + c_E(k),**

where
- c_A(1) = 1, and c_A(k) = 2^(k−1) (k+1)^(k−2) for k ≥ 2;
- c_D(k) = 0 for k ≤ 3, c_D(4) = 104, and c_D(k) = 2^(k−1) U(k) + (k−1) k^(k−2) 2^(k−2) for k ≥ 5, with U(k) = A057500(k), the number of labelled connected unicyclic graphs on k vertices;
- c_E(6) = 355104, c_E(7) = 43733504, c_E(8) = 4053973888, and c_E(k) = 0 otherwise.

c(k) is the number of such matrices of size k that are **connected**, i.e. whose graph G(M) (edges ij with M_ij ≠ 0) is connected.

Notation: a *root* of a lattice is a vector of norm ⟨v, v⟩ = 2.

---

## 1. From matrices to root bases

**Lemma 1.1.** M is such a matrix iff M is the Gram matrix of a basis b_1, …, b_n of an integral lattice L = Zb_1 + … + Zb_n in which every b_i is a root. In that case L is even and generated by roots.

*Proof.* If M is positive definite, M = BᵀB (Cholesky), and the columns b_i of B are linearly independent with ⟨b_i, b_j⟩ = M_ij, so L is integral and ⟨b_i, b_i⟩ = 2. Conversely, a Gram matrix of a basis is positive definite. For two roots r ≠ ±s of any positive definite integral lattice, Cauchy–Schwarz gives |⟨r, s⟩| < 2, so ⟨r, s⟩ ∈ {−1, 0, 1}; distinct basis vectors are never ±each other. L is even, since ⟨Σ aᵢbᵢ, Σ aᵢbᵢ⟩ = 2Σaᵢ² + 2Σ_{i<j} aᵢaⱼM_ij. ∎

**Theorem 1.2 (Witt 1941).** A positive definite even lattice generated by its roots is a *root lattice*, i.e. an orthogonal direct sum of lattices of type A_k (k ≥ 1), D_k (k ≥ 4), E_6, E_7, E_8. The decomposition is unique, and every root lies in exactly one summand.
(See Conway–Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, Ch. 4; Ebeling, *Lattices and Codes*, Thm 1.2.) The irreducible ones are A_k (k ≥ 1), D_k (k ≥ 4), E_6, E_7, E_8, pairwise non-isometric (D_3 ≅ A_3 is not listed twice).

**Lemma 1.3 (counting by lattice).** For a root lattice L of rank n,

  #{matrices M whose lattice L_M is isometric to L} = #{ordered bases of L consisting of roots} / |Aut(L)|.

*Proof.* Map an ordered root basis to its Gram matrix. Two root bases (b_i) and (b′_i) of L have the same Gram matrix iff the linear map b_i ↦ b′_i is an isometry of L, i.e. an element of Aut(L). An automorphism fixing a basis is the identity, so Aut(L) acts freely on ordered root bases and the fibres are exactly the orbits. Every M with L_M ≅ L arises: transport the basis of L_M along an isometry L_M → L. Matrices with non-isometric lattices are different. ∎

**Lemma 1.4 (components).** Let M have lattice L. Then G(M) is connected iff L is irreducible.

*Proof.* By Theorem 1.2, each b_i lies in exactly one irreducible summand L^(t(i)). If M_ij ≠ 0 then b_i and b_j are not orthogonal, so t(i) = t(j). Hence t is constant on each component of G(M).
- If G(M) is connected, all b_i lie in one summand, and since they span L, L is irreducible.
- If G(M) is disconnected, with a component C, the sublattices spanned by {b_i : i ∈ C} and by the other b_i are orthogonal and together span L. So L is reducible. ∎

**Corollary 1.5 (exponential formula).** Relabel the indices by the components of G(M). Then M is block diagonal with connected blocks, and conversely any set partition of {1..n}, with a connected matrix on each block, gives a matrix M. So a(n) = Σ over set partitions of Π c(|B|), i.e. Σ a(n) xⁿ/n! = exp C(x). By Lemmas 1.3 and 1.4,

  c(k) = Σ_{L irreducible of rank k} (ordered root bases of L) / |Aut(L)|.

The automorphism groups are Aut(L) = W(L) ⋊ (diagram symmetries):

| L | \|Aut(L)\| |
|---|---|
| A_1 | 2 |
| A_k (k ≥ 2) | 2(k+1)! |
| D_4 | 3 · 2⁴ · 4! = 1152 |
| D_k (k ≥ 5) | 2ᵏ k! |
| E_6 | 103680 |
| E_7 | 2903040 |
| E_8 | 696729600 |

It remains to count the root bases.

## 2. Type A

A_k = {x ∈ Z^(k+1) : Σxᵢ = 0}, with roots ±(e_i − e_j). A root determines an edge {i, j} of K_(k+1) and an orientation.

**Lemma 2.1.** k roots form a basis of A_k iff their edges form a spanning tree of K_(k+1).

*Proof.*
- **(⇐)** The incidence vectors of a forest are linearly independent: a leaf edge is the only one with a nonzero coordinate at the leaf, so induct. For any i, j, the root e_i − e_j is the signed sum of the tree edges along the path from i to j, so the tree's roots generate every root and hence A_k.
- **(⇒)** Independence rules out cycles, whose signed edge vectors sum to 0, and repeated edges, which give ±the same root. So the k edges form a forest on k + 1 vertices with k edges, i.e. a spanning tree. ∎

By Cayley there are (k+1)^(k−1) spanning trees, and each edge carries one of 2 roots. That gives k!·2ᵏ(k+1)^(k−1) ordered bases, and c_A(k) = k!2ᵏ(k+1)^(k−1)/(2(k+1)!) = 2^(k−1)(k+1)^(k−2) for k ≥ 2. For k = 1: 2 ordered bases / 2 = 1.

## 3. Type D

D_k = {x ∈ Zᵏ : Σxᵢ even} (k ≥ 4), with roots ±e_i ± e_j (i ≠ j).
- Write a root up to sign as x = e_a − σ e_b with σ ∈ {±1}: σ = +1 for e_a − e_b ("type −") and σ = −1 for e_a + e_b ("type +").
- A set S of roots, no two equal up to sign, determines a multigraph Γ(S) on the coordinates {1..k}, with one edge ab of sign σ per root. Parallel edges must have opposite signs.
- A cycle of Γ(S), including a 2-cycle formed by two parallel edges, is **balanced** if the product of its signs is +1, i.e. it has an even number of type-+ edges. Otherwise it is **unbalanced**.

**Lemma 3.1 (rank of a component).** Let Γ′ be a connected component of Γ(S), with vertex set V′ and |V′| ≥ 2. The span of its vectors has dimension |V′| − 1 if every cycle of Γ′ is balanced, and |V′| otherwise.

*Proof.* The vectors lie in R^{V′}.
- **Balanced:** choose ε_v = ±1 with ε_b = σ ε_a along a spanning tree. Balance makes this consistent on every edge. Replacing e_v by ε_v e_v (an isometry) turns every vector into ±(e_a − e_b). These span {x : Σ_{V′} x = 0}, of dimension |V′| − 1 (by §2, they include a spanning tree).
- **Unbalanced:** walk an unbalanced cycle v_0 v_1 … v_m = v_0 with signs σ_1, …, σ_m. Then Σ_t (σ_1⋯σ_(t−1))(e_(v_(t−1)) − σ_t e_(v_t)) = e_(v_0) − (σ_1⋯σ_m) e_(v_0) = 2e_(v_0). So e_(v_0) is in the span. Propagating along edges (e_a − σ e_b and e_a give e_b), the span contains every e_v, v ∈ V′. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (independence).** Let S be k roots of D_k. Then S is linearly independent iff every component of Γ(S) is unicyclic and its cycle is unbalanced.

*Proof.* Components live on disjoint coordinate sets, so S is independent iff each component's vectors are independent. By Lemma 3.1, for a component with v vertices and e edges this means e = v − 1 with all cycles balanced (a tree), or e = v with an unbalanced cycle and no other cycle (a unicyclic graph). Summing over components, e − v is −1 per tree and 0 per unicyclic component, and in total k − k = 0. So there are no tree components. Isolated vertices count as trees with v = 1, e = 0, so they are excluded too. ∎

**Lemma 3.3 (index 1).** Let S be k independent roots of D_k. Then S is a basis of D_k iff Γ(S) is connected.

*Proof.*
- **(⇐)** Γ(S) is connected and unicyclic with an unbalanced cycle. The integer combination in the proof of Lemma 3.1 shows 2e_(v_0) ∈ ZS. Propagating along edges, 2e_v ∈ ZS for all v: from e_a − σ e_b ∈ ZS and 2e_a ∈ ZS we get 2σ e_b. Then for each edge ab both e_a − e_b and e_a + e_b lie in ZS, since they differ from e_a − σ e_b by 0 or 2e_b. By connectivity, every e_i ± e_j is a sum of such edge vectors along a path, plus multiples of 2e_v. So ZS contains every root of D_k and equals D_k, and |S| = k makes it a basis.
- **(⇒)** If Γ(S) has two components on coordinate sets V_1 ⊔ V_2 = {1..k}, every vector of S has even coordinate sum on V_1. So e_a + e_b with a ∈ V_1, b ∈ V_2 is not in ZS, and ZS ≠ D_k. ∎

**Counting.** Choose the typed multigraph Γ and then a sign for each root. Ordered root bases = k!·2ᵏ·T(k), where T(k) is the number of connected typed multigraphs on {1..k} with k edges and an unbalanced unique cycle:
- **cycle of length ≥ 3:** a connected unicyclic simple graph (U(k) choices), with edge types such that the cycle has an odd number of type-+ edges: 2^(k−1) choices;
- **2-cycle:** a spanning tree with one edge doubled ((k−1)k^(k−2) choices). The doubled pair has one edge of each type, so it is automatically unbalanced, and the other k − 2 edges are free: 2^(k−2) choices.

So T(k) = 2^(k−1)U(k) + (k−1)k^(k−2)2^(k−2). With |Aut(D_k)| = 2ᵏk! this gives c_D(k) = T(k) for k ≥ 5, and c_D(4) = T(4)/3 = 312/3 = 104. ∎

### Lean formalization of §§2–3
`lean/OeisLean/A114601.lean` formalizes the lattice characterizations behind §2 and §3. Both theorems use only the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).

- **`A114601.span_eq_D_iff`:** for a set S of signed edges on Fin k, where edge (a, b, s) stands for the root e_a − s·e_b, the roots generate D_k = {x : Σx even} iff every two coordinates are joined by a walk in S and some closed walk has sign product −1. This is Lemmas 3.1–3.3 in walk form: connectivity, plus an unbalanced cycle.
- **`A114601.span_eq_A_iff`:** with all signs +1, the roots e_a − e_b generate {x : Σx = 0} iff the graph is connected (Lemma 2.1).

The proofs follow the text:
- a walk from u to w with sign t puts e_u − t·e_w in the lattice;
- a disconnected graph leaves an invariant parity (D) or sum (A) over the reachable set;
- a balanced graph admits a consistent sign ε, and Σ ε_v x_v vanishes on the lattice but not on 2e_u.

Not formalized:
- Witt's classification (Theorem 1.2);
- the automorphism group orders;
- "k generators of a free ℤ-module of rank k form a basis";
- the counting of trees and unicyclic graphs (Cayley, A057500);
- the three E constants, which are machine computations checked two ways.

## 4. Type E

c_E(k) = (ordered root bases of E_k)/|Aut(E_k)| by Lemma 1.3. These three constants are finite computations, done by two independent methods that agree (see README, step 2):
- enumerating all PD matrices of size k and taking the connected ones of determinant det(E_k) = 3, 2, 1;
- enumerating root subsets of the actual lattices E_6, E_7, E_8 whose Gram determinant is det(E_k). This count is checked by Cauchy–Binet: the sum over all k-subsets of det G_S equals h^k, with Coxeter number h.

This completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

## 5. Closed form of the generating function

Let T(z) = Σ_{m≥1} m^(m−1) zᵐ/m! be the tree function (T = z e^T). Standard EGFs:
- unrooted labelled trees: Σ m^(m−2) zᵐ/m! = T − T²/2;
- trees with a marked edge: Σ (m−1) m^(m−2) zᵐ/m! = T²/2;
- U(z) = Σ U(k) zᵏ/k! = −½ log(1 − T) − T/2 − T²/4 (A057500).

From §2 and §3, with t = T(2x):

  C_A(x) = (t − t²/2 − 2x)/(4x) + x/2,
  C_D(x) = ½ U(2x) + t²/8 − x²/2 − 16x³/3! − 208x⁴/4!.

In C_A, the x/2 corrects the k = 1 term. In C_D, the subtracted terms remove k = 2 and k = 3 (D_2 = A_1², D_3 = A_3) and apply the D_4 triality factor. Then

  **C(x) = C_A(x) + C_D(x) + 355104 x⁶/6! + 43733504 x⁷/7! + 4053973888 x⁸/8!.**

`egf_check.py` expands this closed form as a power series and confirms that it matches c(k) for k ≤ 30.

**Simplification.** In C_D the t²/8 terms cancel: C_D(x) = −¼ log(1 − t) − t/4 − x²/2 − 8x³/3 − 26x⁴/3.

**Asymptotics.** a(n) ~ K · n! · (2e)ⁿ · n^(−7/8), with

  K = exp(e/4 − 3/4 + 1/(4e) − 1/(8e²) − 1/(3e³) − 13/(24e⁴) + Σ_{k=6,7,8} c_E(k)/(k!(2e)ᵏ)) / (2^(1/8) Γ(1/8)) = 0.14730039…

*Derivation.* T(z) has its only dominant singularity at z = 1/e, with 1 − T(z) ~ √2 · (1 − ez)^(1/2). So with t = T(2x), x₀ = 1/(2e):
- the only divergent part of C(x) is −¼ log(1 − t), and exp of it is (1 − t)^(−1/4) ~ 2^(−1/8)(1 − x/x₀)^(−1/8);
- everything else in C is analytic at x₀ up to (1 − x/x₀)^(1/2) terms, which give an O(n^(−1/2)) relative correction. Its value at x₀ (t = 1) is e/4 − 3/4 + 1/(4e) − x₀²/2 − 8x₀³/3 − 26x₀⁴/3 plus the E terms.

Transfer ([xⁿ](1 − x/x₀)^(−α) ~ x₀^(−n) n^(α−1)/Γ(α), with α = 1/8) gives the result.

*Numerical check* (`bfile.py`, and a scaled floating-point recurrence to n = 6400): a(n)/(K n!(2e)ⁿn^(−7/8)) = 0.929, 0.945, 0.970, 0.984, 0.989 at n = 100, 200, 800, 3200, 6400. A fit 1 + b/√n + c/n through n = 1600, 3200, 6400 extrapolates to 1.000007.

**Bumps from E₈.** a(n)/(n·a(n−1)) is not monotone near n = 16: E₈ ⊕ E₈ alone contributes C(16,8)/2 · c_E(8)² ≈ 1.06·10²³ ≈ 40% of a(16). Without the E terms the ratio is smooth. The E part of C is a polynomial, so its effect on the ratios dies out.
