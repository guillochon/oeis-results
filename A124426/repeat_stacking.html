Repeated Sizes: Stacking

N dolls where several can share a size. Dolls of the same size are identical, and only the order of the sizes matters. Pieces nest, and smaller pieces can also stand on flat heads. Every arrangement for N = 1 to 4 is drawn below, grouped by size set.

The rules

  • Each of the N dolls has a size, and sizes can repeat. Only their order matters: sizes 1, 1, 3 are the same set as 1, 1, 2 or 2, 2, 3. So a size set is a list of how many dolls share each size, smallest first, and there are 2N−1 of them.
  • Dolls of the same size are identical: swapping them changes nothing, and any top fits any bottom of its size.
  • Each doll is either closed or split into its two halves.
  • A piece only goes inside or on top of a strictly larger piece. Equal sizes never nest or stack.
  • A closed doll or an open bottom holds one item directly: a smaller closed doll or a smaller bottom (which may hold more).
  • An open top holds one item directly, and only a smaller top. A top never covers a doll or a bottom.
  • A loose top never goes inside a bottom or a closed doll.
  • The flat head of a top half or a closed doll holds one smaller item: a closed doll, a bottom or a top, with whatever it carries.
  • A head shut inside a closed doll or tucked under another top can't hold anything. A piece standing in an open bottom sticks out, so its head is still usable.
  • Position on the table doesn't matter. The count adds up the arrangements over every size set.

Not in the OEIS as of 2026-10-03. The size set with all sizes distinct gives the stacking sequence 2, 19, 312, 7643, …; a single shared size gives N + 1 (just how many dolls are closed). The terms come from Burnside's lemma: average, over the ways of permuting equal pieces, the number of arrangements each permutation leaves unchanged. Those counts reduce to the slot-counting recurrence of the distinct-size case. A brute-force enumeration of every arrangement agrees for N ≤ 4, and a Pólya multiset count agrees for N ≤ 6.
